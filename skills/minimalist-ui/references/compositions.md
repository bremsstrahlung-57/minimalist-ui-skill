# Page compositions

Choose one archetype for each major page or view before building. A project may use each archetype only once: never repeat one within the same project. Pick the closest fit to the page's actual task, then adapt its geometry and content to the product; these are composition starting points, not templates. State the chosen name alongside the dials before implementation.

## Archetypes

1. **Index** — A clear entry point organized as a compact, scannable list of destinations or items. Best when choosing where to go is the main task.
2. **Field Notes** — A reading-led page with a strong title, narrow measure, dated or sourced annotations, and occasional relevant artifacts. Best for documentation, essays, and research notes.
3. **Instrument Panel** — A dense working surface where a few real metrics and controls support ongoing decisions. Best for monitoring or operational tools; prioritize data hierarchy over ornament.
4. **Specimen** — One entity shown in depth, with its defining attributes, evidence, and available actions grouped around it. Best for profiles, product details, records, and results.
5. **Atlas** — A spatial or categorical overview in which position, relationships, or distribution are the primary content. Best when people need to locate, compare, or navigate a set; provide a readable non-visual equivalent.
6. **Queue** — A prioritized sequence of items with explicit state and a clear next action. Best for triage, tasks, review, and inbox-like workflows.
7. **Split Stage** — A focused input or decision area paired with an immediately useful preview or consequence. Best for configuration, search, and creation flows where changing an input should visibly affect an output.
8. **Editorial Spread** — A strong editorial lead paired with a relevant image, artifact, or piece of evidence, followed by a deliberate reading sequence. Best for a campaign, feature, or story whose subject deserves a narrative introduction.
9. **Timeline** — Events arranged along meaningful time, with clear dates, sequence, and cause or status. Best for history, progress, schedules, and incident review.
10. **Workbench** — A hands-on canvas or central artifact surrounded by only the tools and references needed to act on it. Best for editing, analysis, and making tasks where the material itself is the workspace.

## Selection rules

- Choose based on the user's main task and the subject's natural structure, not the page category alone.
- Assign each major page/view a different archetype. If two pages seem to need the same one, distinguish their primary tasks and select a better-fitting composition for at least one; do not duplicate an archetype within the project.
- Keep the archetype in service of real content and working actions. Do not add maps, timelines, dashboards, or workbenches merely to satisfy a label.
- Treat `references/craft.md` hero grammars as a separate choice: a hero grammar can sit inside an archetype and should still be selected to suit the subject.
